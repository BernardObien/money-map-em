Money Map · EM Extension v0.2 · prototype

Corridor rate transparency for emerging markets

An open extension of the MIT DCI Money Map for corridors, methods, and rails where operator-side ground truth changes the picture — mobile-money receive, EM-native remittance providers, stablecoin off-ramps, and P2P channels.

Built by Bernard O'bien · Cambio Last updated: — · Source on GitHub

How to read this. Pick a corridor, set the amount, and compare methods by fee, speed, and what the recipient actually receives. Rows tagged “Missing from DCI” are the coverage gaps this extension surfaces. Click any row to expand its on-ramp / network / off-ramp / FX-spread breakdown.

Methodology & caveats
Prototype note. The current DCI Money Map covers 10 US-originating corridors and shows Nigeria's best fee as 0.00% (data unavailability rendered as zero). This extension adds four corridors, ten+ methods per corridor including mobile-money and P2P rails, and explicit parallel-market FX benchmarking. Rates reflect published fee schedules and typical off-ramp spreads observed in live corridors; NGN bank off-ramp is modeled at retail (~0.1–0.5%), not 2%, and every recipient value is capped below the amount sent. Card on-ramp is differentiated by asset (USDC ~2.44% vs USDT ~4.19%, Coinbase schedule). Network fees are flat, so at larger transfer sizes the effective stablecoin cost falls below the headline percentage. Not live quotes.

Purpose
An open, extensible prototype extending the DCI Money Map to corridors and methods where emerging-market retail users actually transact. Built to inform the September 9 DCI Global talk and as an input into potential collaboration with MIT DCI's Payments Dashboard project.
Methodology
Rates are August 2026 approximations from published provider schedules, off-ramp spreads, and Cambio corridor observations. FX benchmarks use parallel-market rates where a meaningful gap exists (NGN, ARS). Not live quotes — see roadmap for planned live-API integration.
Data: data.json — updated daily. Corrections, additional methods, or corridor requests: obien@mit.edu or open an issue on GitHub.
